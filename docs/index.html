Australian National Parks Tourism

By Nicholas Ong · October 2026

Introduction

This story investigates visitor numbers to Australia’s national parks and protected areas. It compares visitation across parks and across seasons, looks for long-term tourism trends, and explores how outdoor tourism has changed over time.

Why does tourism to National Parks even matter?

It matters because it plays an important role to Australia's economy, environment and local communities.

Most people think that Australia's national parks are
busiest during summer and school holidays.

Let us go through the numbers in visits,
parks and routes, to see whether this is true.

The story moves through five parts:

  1. 01 Establishing the landscape
  2. 02 Change
  3. 03 Regional tourism
  4. 04 Access over time
  5. 05 The business base

Section 01

What does the landscape in Australia look like?

Australia's protected areas differs in size and location.
These maps show where national parks are found,
how much land is protected in each state and territory,
and how governance patterns differ across the country.

Figure 1

More parks east, bigger parks inland

Start with the large circle in inland South Australia.
Circle size show park area, bigger means more hectares.
Many small circles cluster on the east coast;
whereas, the largest parks is located in the interior and north.

Darker circles mark older parks; brighter ones are newer.
Both appear on the coast and interior.
Large, lighter inland circles suggest that some large
areas became national parks recently.
